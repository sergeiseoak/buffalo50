## Gambling Is Based on Chance

Slots such as Buffalo 50 are games of chance.

Features including RTP, volatility, paylines, Wild symbols and Free Games describe how a slot is designed. They do not provide a reliable way to predict the outcome of the next spin.

Previous results also do not make a future result "due."

For example, a sequence of losing spins does not mean that a win must follow. Likewise, triggering Free Games does not indicate that another bonus is more or less likely to appear shortly afterwards.

There is no betting system that can guarantee a profit from a random slot game.

## Set a Budget Before You Play

Decide how much you are prepared to spend **before** starting a real-money session.

That amount should come from money that is not required for essentials such as:

- housing;
- food;
- utilities;
- debt repayments;
- healthcare;
- education;
- savings;
- other financial commitments.

Once the chosen gambling budget has been spent, stop.

Adding more money because you want to recover the original amount changes a predefined entertainment budget into loss chasing.

## Don't Chase Losses

Chasing losses happens when someone continues gambling or increases their bets primarily to recover money already lost.

For example:

**You planned to spend 30 → you lose 30 → you deposit another 30 because you want the first 30 back.**

The second deposit does not have a better mathematical chance simply because the first one was lost.

Past losses don't make future wins more likely.

If you notice yourself increasing your budget specifically to "get even," that is a good reason to stop the session.

## Set a Time Limit as Well as a Money Limit

Money isn't the only useful limit.

Casino games can move quickly, particularly when played on a phone or with faster spin settings. A large number of individual wagers can therefore happen within what feels like a short session.

Decide how long you want to play and take regular breaks.

Avoid gambling when you are:

- exhausted;
- highly stressed;
- angry;
- under the influence of alcohol or drugs;
- trying to distract yourself from a serious personal or financial problem.

These situations can make deliberate decision-making more difficult.

## Understand the Difference Between RTP and Your Results

Buffalo 50 has a published theoretical RTP, but RTP should never be interpreted as a promise that an individual player will receive a particular percentage of their money back.

RTP is calculated over a very large number of game rounds.

Your own session can finish:

above the theoretical return;

below it;

or with the entire gambling budget lost.

The same principle applies to volatility. A volatility classification describes characteristics of the game's mathematical model. It does not guarantee that your particular session will follow a predictable pattern.

## Demo Play Is Not a Predictor

Using a free-play version of a slot can be useful for learning its controls and rules without risking money.

It should not be used to decide that a game is "ready to pay."

For example, none of the following is a reliable signal:

**"I lost 20 demo spins, so I'll switch to real money now."**

**"Free Games just triggered in demo, so the slot is hot."**

**"I haven't seen the Scatter for a long time, so it must appear soon."**

Demo results do not provide a forecast of future real-money results.

## Keep Track of What You Actually Spend

It is easy to remember deposits while overlooking the total amount wagered during a session.

Check your gambling activity periodically and consider recording:

**Deposits** — How much money did you add?

**Withdrawals** — How much did you actually withdraw?

**Net result** — What is the difference between money deposited and money withdrawn over the period you're reviewing?

Looking at actual numbers can provide a clearer picture than relying on memorable wins.

## Warning Signs to Pay Attention To

Problem gambling does not look exactly the same for everyone, but some behaviors deserve attention.

These can include:

- spending more than you intended;
- repeatedly breaking your own gambling limits;
- increasing bets to recover losses;
- borrowing money to gamble;
- using money intended for essential expenses;
- hiding gambling activity or losses from other people;
- feeling anxious, angry or distressed when unable to gamble;
- spending increasing amounts of time thinking about gambling;
- neglecting work, study, relationships or responsibilities;
- continuing despite clear financial or emotional harm;
- treating gambling as a necessary source of income.

You do not need to experience every sign before taking action.

If gambling is causing harm, acting earlier is generally easier than waiting for the situation to become more serious.

## Use Gambling Limits Where Available

Licensed gambling operators may provide responsible-gaming controls. The exact tools available depend on the operator and jurisdiction.

They can include:

### Deposit limits

Restrict how much money can be deposited during a defined period.

### Loss or spending limits

Restrict the amount that can be lost or wagered.

### Session or time limits

Help control how long an account can be used.

### Reality checks

Display reminders showing how long you have been playing.

### Cooling-off periods

Temporarily restrict access to gambling.

### Self-exclusion

Block access for a longer period when a temporary break is not enough.

If you use these tools, choose limits while you're calm rather than waiting until you are already trying to recover a loss.

## What Is Self-Exclusion?

Self-exclusion is intended for people who want to prevent themselves from accessing gambling for a specified period or, depending on the system, indefinitely.

The exact process varies between operators and jurisdictions.

Some countries or regions operate centralized self-exclusion schemes covering multiple licensed gambling businesses. Elsewhere, exclusion may need to be requested separately from individual operators.

If you believe you need self-exclusion, use the official scheme or operator tools applicable where you live.

Trying to bypass your own exclusion by opening new accounts or using different gambling sites defeats the purpose of the protection.

## Blocking Gambling on Your Devices

Operator limits are not the only option.

Specialized blocking software can restrict access to gambling websites and applications on personal devices.

Additional practical steps can include:

removing gambling apps;

unsubscribing from promotional messages;

turning off gambling-related notifications;

blocking gambling payments where your bank provides that option;

asking someone you trust to help you manage finances temporarily.

The right combination depends on your circumstances.

## Getting Support

If gambling is causing financial, emotional or personal harm, consider contacting a professional gambling-support organization.

Because Buffalo50.co is not tied to a specific country, we do not assume that one national service is appropriate for every visitor.

Internationally recognized resources include **Gambling Therapy**, which provides information and support to people affected by problem gambling in multiple languages, and **Gamblers Anonymous**, which operates peer-support groups in numerous locations.

Your local healthcare provider or gambling regulator may also be able to direct you to services available where you live.

If gambling has contributed to an immediate mental-health crisis or you believe you may harm yourself or someone else, contact your local emergency service or crisis service immediately.

## If Someone Else's Gambling Concerns You

Gambling-related harm can affect partners, relatives and friends as well as the person gambling.

You cannot control another person's decisions, but you can:

talk about specific behavior you've noticed;

avoid lending money for gambling;

protect shared finances where appropriate;

seek confidential advice for yourself;

encourage the person to contact professional support.

Support organizations may also provide resources specifically for family members and affected others.

## Protect Children and Underage Users

Real-money gambling is not for children.

If you share a device with a child or teenager:

- don't leave a gambling account signed in;
- protect payment methods;
- use device passwords or biometric security;
- don't share account credentials;
- consider parental controls and website restrictions.

Adults should also avoid presenting gambling as an easy way to earn money.

## Keep Gambling Separate From Income

A casino balance should not be treated like a salary, an investment account, savings, a debt-repayment plan or an emergency fund.

Even if a session ends with a win, future results remain uncertain.

Gambling should only come from a predefined entertainment budget where losing the entire amount would not interfere with everyday financial obligations.

## A Simple Check Before Playing

Before starting a real-money session, ask yourself:

**Can I afford to lose the entire amount I'm about to spend?**

**Have I decided when I will stop?**

**Am I playing for entertainment rather than to recover money?**

**Am I comfortable stopping if I lose?**

**Am I legally old enough and permitted to gamble where I live?**

If the answer to any of these questions is no, don't start the session.
