How to Play Buffalo 50: Rules, Paylines and Bonus Features

Buffalo 50 gameplay
Stacked Wilds

Learning how to play Buffalo 50 starts with a fairly traditional slot setup. The Endorphina game uses 5 reels, 4 rows and 50 fixed paylines. You choose a stake, spin the reels and receive a payout when qualifying symbols form a winning combination according to the paytable.

The two symbols worth identifying immediately are the Buffalo Wild and Golden Coin Scatter. The buffalo can substitute for regular symbols and appear stacked, while three Golden Coins trigger 5 Free Games.

Buffalo 50 has a published 96.06% RTP and low volatility. Those figures describe its mathematical model; they don't provide a method for predicting individual spins.

The paytable is effectively the rulebook built into the slot.

Open it before you start spinning.

It shows which symbols produce payouts, how many matching symbols are needed and which icons have special functions.

This is more reliable than guessing from the artwork.

A large animal symbol isn't automatically valuable simply because it looks important, and the Golden Coin works differently from a standard paying symbol because it acts as the Scatter.

The paytable also helps answer a common question after a spin:

Why did this combination win while another apparently similar combination didn't?

Usually, the answer is in the symbol requirements and payline pattern.

Step 3: Understand What “50 Paylines” Means

This is the mechanic behind the game's name.

Buffalo 50 uses 50 fixed paylines.

A payline is a predefined path across the reels. It can run straight across a row or move through different positions as it crosses the grid.

Because the game has 50 of these patterns, several different lines can potentially participate in the same spin.

But there are two misconceptions to avoid.

50 paylines does not mean 50 wins per spin.

And:

50 paylines does not mean you have a 50% chance of winning.

The number simply describes how many predefined patterns are used to evaluate qualifying symbol combinations.

Step 4: Set Your Stake

Before pressing Spin, find the betting control and confirm the total amount being wagered per spin.

Don't rely on the number of paylines to estimate the cost.

The interface should show the actual stake associated with the next round.

For example, if the displayed total bet is 1 unit, that's the figure that matters for your budget. You shouldn't automatically multiply it by 50 unless the interface explicitly tells you that you're looking at a per-line amount.

This is particularly important when switching between different slot games because betting controls are not always presented in exactly the same way.

Choose the stake before the spin

Changing the stake after a loss doesn't alter what the next random result is going to be.

A practical approach is to decide in advance:

session budget ÷ approximate number of spins = suitable average stake.

For example, if you have set aside 40 units and want that budget to cover roughly 100 spins, a 2-unit stake clearly doesn't fit that plan.

That's budgeting, not a winning strategy. It simply gives you more control over how quickly money can be spent.

Step 5: Spin the Reels

Once the stake is set, pressing Spin starts a game round.

The reels stop on a new arrangement of symbols, and Buffalo 50 checks the result against its paytable and 50 paylines.

If a valid combination occurs, the interface displays the corresponding win.

If no qualifying combination occurs, the stake for that spin is lost.

There is no extra action you can take while the reels are spinning that changes the underlying result.

Stopping animations early, tapping the screen or changing devices doesn't create a better outcome.

Step 6: Learn What the Buffalo Wild Does

The buffalo is the game's Wild symbol.

Its purpose is to substitute for standard symbols when doing so helps form a valid winning combination.

For example, imagine a qualifying line containing:

Eagle – Eagle – Buffalo – Eagle – Eagle

If the Wild can substitute for the missing Eagle in that position, the game can evaluate the combination accordingly.

The Buffalo Wild does not substitute for the Golden Coin Scatter.

Buffalo Wilds can be stacked

The Wild isn't limited to appearing as a single buffalo.

It can appear stacked vertically on a reel.

That matters because a stack can occupy several positions and potentially participate in multiple paylines.

But don't confuse potential with certainty.

A full or partial Wild stack doesn't automatically create a payout. It still needs to connect with appropriate symbols according to the game's rules.

Step 7: Watch for the Golden Coin Scatter

The Golden Coin has a different role.

It is the Buffalo 50 Scatter symbol.

Landing three Golden Coins triggers the Free Games feature.

Three Scatters also award a payout equal to 2× the base bet.

Unlike an ordinary symbol that depends on a standard payline combination, a Scatter is tied to its own triggering rules.

This is why you shouldn't treat the Buffalo and Golden Coin as interchangeable “bonus symbols”:

Buffalo → Wild

Golden Coin → Scatter

They perform different jobs.

Step 8: Trigger 5 Free Games

Three Golden Coin Scatters award 5 Free Games.

Once the feature begins, the game provides the awarded spins without charging a separate normal stake for each one.

The same general 5×4 structure remains recognizable during the feature.

Stacked Buffalo Wilds can still play a role, making them particularly noticeable when several buffalo positions appear across the reels.

The important point is that five spins is the initial award, not necessarily the maximum possible length of the feature.

Can Buffalo 50 Free Games Be Retriggered?

Yes.

The Free Games feature can be retriggered.

That means the appropriate Scatter event during the feature can award additional Free Games rather than forcing the bonus to stop after the original five.

A retrigger doesn't mean the feature is entering a guaranteed profitable phase.

The additional spins are still governed by the game's mathematics.

More spins create more game rounds. They don't guarantee that those rounds will produce a specific payout.

Step 9: Decide Whether to Use the Risk Game

Buffalo 50 includes a Risk Game that can become available after an eligible win.

This creates a decision:

keep the existing win

or

risk it for the chance to increase it.

The provider describes the feature as offering the possibility to double a win, with repeated use available up to ten times.

The Risk Game is optional.

That's important because some players treat a gamble button as though it were the next required stage of the slot.

It isn't.

If you already have a result you're satisfied with, keeping it rather than risking it is part of the available gameplay.

Understand the downside before using Risk

Suppose you have a 10-unit eligible win.

Choosing the Risk Game means that existing 10-unit result is being exposed to another uncertain outcome.

The possibility of increasing the win exists because the possibility of losing the amount being risked also exists.

There is no sequence of previous reel results that tells you when the Risk Game is “due” to succeed.

Buffalo 50 RTP Explained

Buffalo 50 has an official published RTP of 96.06%.

RTP stands for Return to Player.

It is a theoretical percentage based on long-term game mathematics, not a cashback rate attached to your personal session.

If you make 20 spins, there's no rule requiring the slot to return 96.06% of your wagers.

The same applies to 100 spins.

Short-term results can vary substantially from the theoretical figure.

So RTP is useful for describing and comparing the mathematical profile of a slot, but it can't answer:

Will my next spin win?

It can't.

What Low Volatility Means

Buffalo 50 is classified as a low-volatility game.

Volatility concerns the way results are distributed.

It should not be translated into:

low volatility = can't lose much.

Your stake can still be lost on any unsuccessful paid spin, and a sequence of losing rounds can occur.

The classification is better understood in relation to the game's overall mathematical behaviour across a large sample of rounds.

RTP and volatility should therefore be read together, but not confused:

Should You Change Your Bet After a Loss?

Not because you think a win is “due.”

Consider this sequence:

loss → loss → loss → loss

It can feel as though the fifth spin should have a better chance.

But the previous four results don't create a debt that the next spin has to repay.

Increasing the stake after losses can instead make the next unsuccessful spin more expensive.

If you change the bet, do it because you're deliberately changing your budget—not because you're trying to force the reels to compensate for previous results.

Does a Wild Stack Mean You Should Raise the Next Bet?

No.

The same principle applies after a visually strong result.

A stacked Buffalo appearing on one spin does not tell you where Wilds will land next.

Neither does a Free Games trigger.

Don't use previous reel layouts as signals for increasing or decreasing the next wager.

Can You Predict the Golden Coin Scatter?

There is no reliable spin-count method for predicting when the next three Golden Coins will appear.

Claims such as:

“Free Spins usually come after 50 spins”

or

“raise your bet after two Scatters”

should not be treated as game rules unless they are explicitly part of the provider's documented mechanics.

Buffalo 50's actual rule is straightforward:

three Golden Coin Scatters trigger five Free Games.

It does not include a public countdown telling the player when that trigger must occur.

Auto Play and Faster Spins Don't Improve the Odds

If a version of the game offers automated or faster play controls, they change the pace of interaction, not the mathematical advantage of a spin.

Playing faster can actually create a practical downside: you can move through a budget more quickly.

For example:

50 spins at a given stake cost the same total amount whether they take several minutes or much longer.

Speed doesn't improve the expected result.

It simply reduces the time between wagering decisions.

How to Read a Buffalo 50 Result

When the reels stop, don't look only at the final win number.

If you're learning the game, check:

Which symbols matched?

Which payline was involved?

Did a Buffalo act as a substitute?

Was more than one payline involved?

Did Golden Coins trigger the Scatter rules?

Understanding those answers makes future results much easier to follow.

This is particularly useful during Buffalo 50 free play, where you can inspect rounds without putting real money at risk.
